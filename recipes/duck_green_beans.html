Duck Breast with Fig Gastrique

serves 2 · ~30 min

For the duck

  • 2 duck breasts (~200 g / 7 oz each), skin on
  • Kosher salt & black pepper

For the gastrique

  • 1 small shallot, minced
  • 6 fresh figs, quartered (or 8–10 dried, soaked 10 min)
  • ¼ cup (60 ml) balsamic vinegar
  • 2 tbsp honey
  • ½ cup (120 ml) chicken or duck stock
  • 1 sprig fresh thyme
  • 2 tbsp port or dry red wine (optional)
  • 1 tbsp (15 g) cold unsalted butter, cubed

Method, in prose: Score the duck skin in a crosshatch and season both sides. Lay skin-side down in a cold, dry skillet and bring the heat up to medium — starting cold renders the fat gradually so the skin turns crisp without seizing. After 8–10 minutes, once the skin is deep gold, flip and cook 4–6 minutes more to 135°F for medium-rare. Rest, tented, 5–10 minutes. While the duck rests, pour off all but a tablespoon of the rendered fat. Sauté the shallot, then deglaze with balsamic and honey, scraping up the fond, and reduce until syrupy. Add stock, thyme, and port and simmer until it coats a spoon. Stir in the figs just long enough to soften, then take the pan off the heat and swirl in the cold butter for shine. Slice the duck on the bias, fan it out, and spoon the gastrique over.

Garlicky Green Beans

serves 3–4 as a side · ~10 min

Beans

  • 1 lb (450 g) green beans, trimmed
  • Kosher salt, to taste
  • Squeeze of lemon juice (optional)
  • 2 tbsp toasted sliced almonds or pine nuts (optional)

Garlic oil

  • 2 tbsp (30 ml) olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • ¼–½ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)

Method, in prose: Blanch the trimmed beans in generously salted, boiling water for 3–4 minutes, until bright green and just tender. Shock in ice water to stop the cooking, then drain and pat dry — dry beans sear instead of steaming later. While the beans blanch, slice the garlic thin. Heat the olive oil over medium, add the garlic, and cook 30–60 seconds until fragrant but not browned (add the pepper flakes here if using). Add the beans, toss to coat, and sauté 2–3 minutes until heated through with a little char. Finish with salt, a squeeze of lemon, and the toasted nuts if using.
Plating together: the beans can sit under or beside the sliced duck, with the gastrique spooned over both — the garlic and the fig gastrique don't compete, since one is savory-sharp and the other is sweet-tannic.
